# Protocol 03: Emergency Signs and Immediate Referral (ESC)

Disclaimer: Educational and operational decision support for trained frontline health workers. Protocols are simplified hackathon materials and are not for clinical use. This system does not replace clinical judgment or qualified medical care.

---

### ESC-1.1: Maternal Danger Signs During Pregnancy
Any of the following signs during pregnancy constitutes an obstetric emergency requiring immediate referral to Chandrapuri District Hospital:
- Vaginal bleeding (spotting, fresh bleeding, clots, or placenta praevia suspicion).
- Severe headache with blurred vision, seeing spots, or epigastric pain (signs of impending eclampsia).
- Convulsions, fits, or loss of consciousness (eclampsia).
- High blood pressure (Systolic BP >= 160 mmHg or Diastolic BP >= 110 mmHg, or >= 140/90 mmHg with symptoms).
- High fever (temperature > 101°F / 38.3°C) with chills or rigors.
- Severe continuous abdominal pain or rigid abdomen.
- Reduced or absent fetal movements after 24 weeks / 5th month of gestation.
- Foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
- Severe breathlessness, difficulty breathing, or severe paleness of tongue/palms (severe anaemia).

### ESC-1.2: Maternal Danger Signs During Labour and Delivery
Immediate emergency referral is required when any of the following occur:
- Prolonged labour: Labour pains lasting longer than 12 hours without progress.
- Premature or prolonged rupture of membranes (PROM): Water breaks more than 18 hours before delivery or foul-smelling amniotic fluid.
- Cord prolapse: Umbilical cord visible or protruding from the vagina (place mother in knee-chest position immediately).
- Abnormal presentation: Hand, foot, or breech presenting first instead of head.
- Retained placenta: Placenta not delivered within 30 minutes after baby's birth.
- Severe vaginal bleeding during or immediately following labour.
- Fits, convulsions, or maternal collapse during delivery.

### ESC-1.3: Maternal Postpartum Danger Signs (After Delivery)
During the 42 days following delivery, watch for:
- Severe Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH): Bleeding that soaks more than two sanitary pads in one hour, or sudden large gush of blood.
- High fever (>101°F / 38.3°C) with chills, shivering, or foul-smelling lochia (puerperal sepsis).
- Severe, unbearable lower abdominal pain.
- Fits, seizures, or loss of consciousness.
- Severe unilateral calf pain, swollen red calf, or sudden chest pain with breathlessness (deep vein thrombosis / pulmonary embolism).
- Severe postnatal depression, agitation, delusions, suicidal thoughts, or wanting to harm the infant (postpartum psychosis).

### ESC-2.1: Newborn and Young Infant Danger Signs (Birth to 2 Months)
A young infant with ANY of the following signs must be referred IMMEDIATELY to Chandrapuri District Hospital Special Newborn Care Unit (SNCU):
- Inability to feed or breastfeed, or stopping feeding completely.
- Convulsions, fits, or abnormal twitching.
- Fast breathing: Breathing rate 60 breaths per minute or faster.
- Severe chest indrawing (lower chest wall goes deep in when baby breathes in).
- High fever (axillary temperature >= 37.5°C / 99.5°F) or hypothermia (body feels cold, temperature < 35.5°C / 95.9°F).
- Lethargy, floppiness, unusually difficult to wake, or unconsciousness.
- Yellow palms and soles, or deep jaundice appearing in first 24 hours of life.
- Umbilical redness spreading to surrounding abdominal skin, or draining foul pus.
- Numerous skin pustules or large bullae/blisters.
- Continuous grunting or wheezing while breathing.

### ESC-2.2: Child Danger Signs (2 Months to 5 Years)
Any child presenting with General Danger Signs must be referred urgently to the nearest First Referral Unit (FRU) / Chandrapuri District Hospital:
- Inability to drink, breastfeed, or take any oral fluids.
- Vomits everything (cannot keep anything down).
- Convulsions or seizures during the current illness.
- Lethargic, abnormally sleepy, or unconscious.
- Stridor in a calm child (harsh rasping sound on breathing in).
- Severe acute malnutrition with bilateral pitting edema of both feet.
- Extremely pale conjunctiva, palms, or tongue (life-threatening anaemia).
- Stiff neck with high fever (suspected meningitis).

### ESC-2.3: Immediate Referral Actions and Transport Protocols
When an emergency danger sign is identified:
1. Explain urgency calmly to the family; do not delay departure.
2. Call 108 Emergency Ambulance or 102 Janani Express immediately.
3. Keep the patient warm (wrap baby with warm cloth / Kangaroo Mother Care; cover mother).
4. If convulsions or vomiting occur: Turn patient on their side to prevent choking and aspiration; clear airway; NEVER put spoon or fingers in mouth; NEVER give fluids orally.
5. In cord prolapse: Keep woman in knee-chest or elevated pelvis position with sterile cloth supporting cord; do not push cord back inside.
6. Carry MCP card, referral slip with recorded vitals and time of onset, and accompany the patient to Chandrapuri District Hospital.
