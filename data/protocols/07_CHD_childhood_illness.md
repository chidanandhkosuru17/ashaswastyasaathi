# Protocol 07: Common Childhood Illnesses Management (CHD)

Disclaimer: Educational and operational decision support for trained frontline health workers. Protocols are simplified hackathon materials and are not for clinical use. This system does not replace clinical judgment or qualified medical care.

---

### CHD-1.1: Acute Diarrhoea and Dehydration Management
Classification of dehydration:
- No Dehydration: Alert, normal eyes, drinks normally, skin pinch goes back immediately. Manage at home with ORS and extra fluids.
- Some Dehydration: Restless/irritable, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly/thirsty, skin pinch goes back slowly (< 2 seconds). Administer ORS at health sub-centre (approx. 75 ml/kg over 4 hours).
- Severe Dehydration: Lethargic or unconscious, sunken eyes, unable to drink or drinks poorly, skin pinch goes back very slowly (> 2 seconds). Emergency referral for IV fluids (Ringer Lactate) immediately.
- ORS Preparation: Dissolve entire standard packet of Oral Rehydration Salts (20.5g WHO formula) in 1 litre of clean drinking water. Mix well. Use within 24 hours; discard leftover solution after 24 hours.
- Zinc Supplementation: Give daily for 14 continuous days, even after diarrhoea stops:
  - Infants under 6 months: 10 mg elemental zinc per day (half tablet dissolved in expressed breast milk).
  - Children 6 months to 5 years: 20 mg elemental zinc per day (one full tablet dissolved in clean water or breast milk).

### CHD-1.2: Acute Respiratory Infection (ARI) and Pneumonia
Fast Breathing Thresholds (count breaths for 1 full minute when child is calm):
- Age < 2 months: 60 breaths per minute or faster.
- Age 2 months to 11 months: 50 breaths per minute or faster.
- Age 12 months to 59 months: 40 breaths per minute or faster.
- Classification and Action:
  - No Pneumonia (Cough/Cold): Normal breathing rate, no chest indrawing. Home care: soothing home fluids (tulsi, ginger, warm water), continue feeding, return immediately if danger signs appear.
  - Pneumonia: Fast breathing, but NO chest indrawing and no general danger signs. Oral antibiotic (Amoxicillin dispersible tablets as per national IMNCI guidelines) prescribed by ANM/MO.
  - Severe Pneumonia: Chest indrawing (lower chest wall moves inward during inspiration) or any general danger sign (stridor, convulsions, inability to drink). Give first dose of antibiotic and REFER IMMEDIATELY to Chandrapuri District Hospital.

### CHD-1.3: Fever and Malaria Screening
- Assess for malaria: In endemic areas, perform Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) for malaria on any child with fever (> 37.5°C / 99.5°F).
- Temperature Management: For high fever (> 38.5°C / 101.3°F), administer Paracetamol syrup/drops (10-15 mg/kg body weight per dose) every 4-6 hours (maximum 4 doses in 24 hours). Perform tepid sponging with normal temperature water; NEVER use ice or cold water.
- Danger signs in fever: Stiff neck, petechial purpuric rash, convulsions, inability to drink, persistent vomiting -> Emergency referral.
