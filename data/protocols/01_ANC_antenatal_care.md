# Protocol 01: Antenatal Care (ANC) Guidelines for Frontline Health Workers

Disclaimer: Educational and operational decision support for trained frontline health workers. Protocols are simplified hackathon materials and are not for clinical use. This system does not replace clinical judgment or qualified medical care.

---

### ANC-1.1: Registration and First ANC Visit
- Every pregnant woman must be registered as early as pregnancy is suspected or confirmed, ideally within the first 12 weeks (first trimester) from the Last Menstrual Period (LMP).
- Issue the Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card immediately upon registration.
- Record baseline weight, blood pressure, hemoglobin (Hb), urine albumin/sugar, and blood grouping.
- Calculate Expected Date of Delivery (EDD) = LMP + 9 months + 7 days (or 280 days).

### ANC-1.2: ANC Visit Schedule
A minimum of four comprehensive antenatal visits are mandated under national guidelines:
1. First ANC Visit: Within 12 weeks of pregnancy (first trimester).
2. Second ANC Visit: Between 14 and 26 weeks of pregnancy (second trimester).
3. Third ANC Visit: Between 28 and 34 weeks of pregnancy (third trimester).
4. Fourth ANC Visit: Between 36 weeks and delivery/term.
- Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA): Organized on the 9th of every month at designated health facilities for specialist check-ups and ultrasound screening.

### ANC-1.3: Td Immunisation in Pregnancy
- Tetanus and adult Diphtheria (Td) vaccine replaces the older TT vaccine.
- Td-1: Administer as early in pregnancy as possible.
- Td-2: Administer 4 weeks (28 days) after Td-1.
- Td Booster: If the pregnant woman received 2 doses of Td/TT in a pregnancy within the previous 3 years, only one Td booster dose is required in the current pregnancy.
- Route and dose: 0.5 ml intramuscularly (IM) in the upper arm (deltoid).

### ANC-1.4: Iron and Folic Acid (IFA) and Calcium Supplementation
- Folic Acid: 1 tablet (400 mcg folic acid) daily during the first trimester (up to 12 weeks) to prevent neural tube defects.
- Iron and Folic Acid (IFA): 1 tablet (100 mg elemental iron + 500 mcg folic acid) daily starting from the 14th week of pregnancy (second trimester) for a minimum of 180 days during pregnancy and continuing for 180 days postpartum.
- In severe anaemia (Hb < 7 g/dL), double IFA dose or refer immediately.
- Calcium Supplementation: Two 500 mg elemental calcium tablets daily (total 1000 mg) starting from 14 weeks onwards until 6 months postpartum.
- Important counseling: IFA and Calcium tablets must NEVER be swallowed together; take Calcium with meals and IFA at bedtime or between meals with lemon water or plain water. Avoid taking IFA with milk, tea, or coffee.

### ANC-1.5: High-Risk Pregnancy (HRP) Identification
Any pregnant woman with one or more of the following conditions is classified as High-Risk Pregnancy:
- Severe anaemia: Hemoglobin < 7 g/dL.
- Hypertension: Systolic BP >= 140 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP >= 90 mmHg.
- Age: Below 18 years or above 35 years.
- Short stature: Height < 140 cm.
- Previous obstetric complications: Previous Caesarean section, stillbirth, recurrent abortion, postpartum haemorrhage (PPH).
- Multiple gestation: Twin or triplet pregnancy.
- Medical disorders: Pre-existing or gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), heart disease, kidney disease, HIV/TB positive.
- Action: Flag MCP card with red sticker, notify ANM/Medical Officer, schedule home follow-up visits every 14 days, and arrange institutional delivery at Chandrapuri District Hospital.

### ANC-1.6: Birth Preparedness and Complication Readiness
- Identify delivery facility: Nearest 24x7 Functional Primary Health Centre (PHC) or Chandrapuri District Hospital.
- Arrange emergency transport: Save contact numbers for 108 (Emergency Ambulance) and 102 (Janani Express).
- Identify two potential blood donors with compatible blood group.
- Save funds for delivery-related out-of-pocket expenses.
- Prepare clean delivery kit and newborn essentials (two clean dry towels, warm clothes, cord ties).
