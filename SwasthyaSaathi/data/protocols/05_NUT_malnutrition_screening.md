# Protocol 05: Malnutrition Screening and Management Guidelines (NUT)

Disclaimer: Educational and operational decision support for trained frontline health workers. Protocols are simplified hackathon materials and are not for clinical use. This system does not replace clinical judgment or qualified medical care.

---

### NUT-1.1: Anthropometric Assessment
- Weight-for-Age: Plotted on WHO growth charts (Green = Normal, Yellow = Moderately Underweight, Red = Severely Underweight).
- Height/Length-for-Age: Measures stunting (chronic malnutrition).
- Weight-for-Height/Length: Measures wasting (acute malnutrition). A standard deviation score below -3 SD denotes Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).

### NUT-1.2: Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) Screening for 6 to 59 Months
MUAC tape is used on the left arm midway between the tip of the shoulder (acromion) and tip of the elbow (olecranon):
- Red Zone (< 11.5 cm / 115 mm): Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). Immediate referral or community management protocol required.
- Yellow Zone (11.5 cm to 12.4 cm / 115 to 124 mm): Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). Targeted supplemental nutrition and bi-weekly counseling.
- Green Zone (>= 12.5 cm / 125 mm): Normal nutritional status. Routine growth monitoring.
- Frequency: Monthly MUAC screening for SAM/MAM children; quarterly screening for all children aged 6 to 59 months.

### NUT-1.3: Bilateral Pitting Edema Assessment
- Press both thumbs firmly but gently on the top of both feet (dorsum of feet) for 3 seconds, then release.
- If a pit/indentation remains on both feet simultaneously, bilateral pitting edema is present.
- Bilateral pitting edema indicates Kwashiorkor (severe acute malnutrition) regardless of MUAC measurement, requiring urgent referral.

### NUT-1.4: Nutritional Rehabilitation Centre (NRC) Referral Criteria
A child aged 6-59 months must be referred to the Chandrapuri NRC under the following conditions:
1. SAM with medical complications (fever, hypothermia, pneumonia, severe diarrhoea, vomiting).
2. SAM with failed Appetite Test (unable or unwilling to eat Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food / energy-dense paste).
3. SAM with bilateral pitting edema (Grade +++ or generalized edema).
4. Any infant under 6 months of age with severe wasting or unable to breastfeed.
- Treatment at NRC includes stabilized therapeutic feeds (F-75, F-100), medical care, micronutrient supplementation, and sensory stimulation.

### NUT-1.5: Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) & Complementary Feeding
- At 6 completed months (180 days), breast milk alone is no longer sufficient; introduce semi-solid mashed complementary food.
- Frequency: 2-3 meals daily at 6-8 months; 3-4 meals daily at 9-23 months, plus 1-2 nutritious snacks (banana, boiled egg, roasted gram, khichdi enriched with oil/ghee).
- Consistency: Thick porridge/khichdi that does not drip easily from the spoon.
- Continue frequent, on-demand breastfeeding until at least 2 years of age or beyond.
